# Product copy for Sean

Help readers understand what the product does, why it is useful to them and what to do next. Marketing material is the primary use case. Build the message from the actual product and audience.

## Learn the product before writing

Use the brief and available project material first. Read relevant product documentation, existing copy and the implementation behind claims when available. Keep the search focused on the requested deliverable. Ask only for missing information that would materially change the message.

Establish the audience, product purpose, real capabilities, intended action, placement and language. Distinguish current functionality, planned functionality, sample data and measured results. Existing copy is evidence of prior wording, not automatic approval of its style or claims. Sean's latest corrections take precedence.

Do not delay a useful draft over minor unknowns. State a consequential assumption briefly outside the copy. If a claim depends on missing evidence, use a supportable alternative or flag the missing fact. Source inspection alone does not establish that a feature is live or works in production.

## Voice and clarity

- Use concise, natural English. Keep the words needed to convey a complete meaning. Minimal wording must still tell the reader something useful.
- Name the product action or benefit concretely. Prefer familiar terms such as M-Pesa messages, spending categories, website, cost and timeline to abstract promises.
- Warmth and personality are welcome when they fit the product. Serious operational tools need a calmer voice. Do not make every product playful.
- Avoid generic slogans, forced wordplay, inflated claims and vague emotional promises. Do not assume the reader feels guilty, overwhelmed or anxious without evidence from the brief or audience.
- Do not use em dashes or rhetorical constructions such as "We're not just X, we're Y."
- Avoid stacks of clipped fragments and formulaic two-part slogans. Sean rejects phrasing such as "A few taps, done" and "Every send sorted." Brevity alone is not quality.
- Two sentences are allowed when both communicate useful information. A headline and supporting sentence are allowed. Do not interpret the rejection of slogan-like rhythm as a ban on normal sentences or short functional labels.
- Prefer concrete verbs to vague terms such as unlock, elevate, seamless and reimagine. Technical terminology can stay when the intended reader understands it and it names a real capability.
- Use genuine numbers when relevant and supported. Never invent metrics, testimonials, customer counts, savings, precision, availability or guarantees.

## Language and Kenyan context

English is the default, including for Kenyan products. An occasional conversational Swahili phrase mixed with clear English may suit the audience. Do not write fully Swahili copy by default. If unsure whether an expression is natural or appropriate, use English.

Do not add Sheng, slang, jokes or local references merely to signal Kenyan identity. Use local context through the actual task, familiar services and relevant examples. Preserve product and service names accurately. Follow an existing English spelling convention; Sean has not specified a universal British-versus-American rule.

## Match the placement

**Websites and landing pages:** Make the product and its benefit understandable from the main message. Supporting copy should explain the mechanism, use case or evidence. Build sections around questions the reader needs answered. Do not impose a generic long landing-page template on a small request.

**Social carousels:** Give the opening slide a clear product benefit, use the middle slides to explain distinct capabilities or steps, and close with an appropriate next action. Honour the requested slide count. Each slide should make sense when read on its own and move the overall explanation forward. Read the approved six-slide Corral example in the voice reference when useful.

**Ads:** Give the intended audience a concrete reason to care, then an appropriate next action. Match the destination page and the actual offer. Do not manufacture urgency or imply guaranteed outcomes.

**App store screenshots:** Give each screenshot a distinct, useful message about the capability shown. Use natural phrases or sentences that can be read at a glance. Supporting text is optional and must add information. Sequence the set from the core use case to supporting benefits; avoid repeating the same vague promise across slides.

**App Store and Play Store listings:** Explain the core use case early. Use confirmed features and platform availability. Treat short descriptions, full descriptions and graphic overlays as different placements. Verify current platform limits when submission-ready text is requested; never assume one universal character limit.

**Play Store feature graphics or other promotional artwork:** Confirm which asset is meant when ambiguous. Write one clear message that works with the visual. Do not turn the graphic into a feature list or claim a platform requirement without checking it.

**Interface copy, when requested:** Preserve the product's terminology. Buttons name actions; statuses report what happened; errors give an accurate next step. Keep permissions, data handling and destructive actions plain and explicit. Do not imply a retry, undo or recovery option exists unless it does.

## Draft and review

Read [voice examples](references/voice-examples.md) when calibrating wording or revising a rejected draft.

Before delivering, check:

1. Can the intended reader tell what this means without decoding a slogan?
2. Does each line communicate a real action, capability, benefit or next step?
3. Is every factual claim supported at the level stated?
4. Does the language fit this audience, product and placement?
5. Did shortening the copy remove necessary meaning or create artificial fragments?

Return the requested copy first, organised by placement. Give alternatives when requested or when a meaningful tone choice remains; do not bury the copy beneath rationale. Flag material assumptions or unverified claims briefly. Preserve approved copy outside the requested edit. A writing request does not authorise publishing, launching ads or submitting store listings.
