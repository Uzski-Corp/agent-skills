# Voice examples and evidence

These examples record Sean's interview preferences. Accepted directions establish taste; they are not proof of released functionality or a requirement to reuse the exact words.

## Accepted direction

For an M-Pesa message analytics tool, Sean preferred a mix of:

- "See where your M-Pesa money goes."
- "Pesa imeenda wapi? See your M-Pesa spending by category."

English should be used most of the time. Conversational Swahili may appear within English copy when natural. Sean explicitly rejected fully Swahili copy as the default.

## Rejected patterns

| Example | What to change |
| --- | --- |
| "No red, no guilt" | Assumes an emotional problem and gives little product information. |
| "The whole month, one scroll" | Compresses the message into a slogan without explaining what the reader can see. |
| "Know your bread" | Forces a brand pun at the expense of product meaning. |
| "Your month simplified" | Leaves the benefit unspecified. |
| "A few taps, done" | Omits the task and substitutes a clipped rhythm for information. |
| "Every send sorted" | Uses vague, unnatural wording and an absolute claim. |
| "We're not just a digital agency, we're crafting..." | Uses the contrast construction Sean explicitly rejects. |

Do not repair these by swapping in synonyms. Return to the actual capability and describe its value to the intended reader.

## Approved Bread screenshot direction

Sean approved this set as much closer to his intended voice. These are copy examples, not verification of current product capabilities.

| Screen | Headline | Supporting copy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly overview | See where your M-Pesa money goes | View your spending by category and see what's left for the month. |
| Add expense | Keep track of what you spend | Add an expense with its amount, category and a note. |
| Categorise transfers | Remember what you sent money for | Give each transfer a category, from family support to eating out. |
| Activity | Find a payment in your spending history | Browse your transactions by day or filter by category. |
| Person's history | See how much you've sent to someone | View their total and revisit individual payments. |

## Approved Corral carousel direction

Sean approved this six-slide introduction as a useful starting point he can refine. Preserve the clear explanation of each step, rather than treating the wording as a fixed template. Reconfirm capabilities and access status before publishing.

1. **Find companies that need what you sell**
   Corral helps you research prospects, find decision-makers and prepare your outreach.
2. **Start with your product**
   Add your website or pitch deck so Corral can understand your offer and who it's for.
3. **See why a company is a good fit**
   Corral researches each company and shows the evidence behind its fit score.
4. **Give people a reason to reply**
   Prepare an introduction that connects your offer to something relevant about their business.
5. **Keep control of your outreach**
   Launch email sequences from your mailbox. Review drafted responses when replies come in.
6. **Get early access to Corral**
   Join the waitlist with your work email. We'll let you know when your workspace is ready.

## Transfer the principles to future products

Learn the actual product, audience and release status before adapting these examples. A demo screen establishes the pictured interaction, not production readiness. A marketing page establishes existing wording, not measured results. Personal or team projects do not automatically establish a paid client relationship. Keep internal source-review notes outside public-facing copy.
